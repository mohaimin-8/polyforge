PolyForge · Thesis Artifact · 2026

Stop scaling one knob at a time.

Clusters that serve classic app traffic and AI inference from the same tenants are still managed by autoscalers that see one knob. PolyForge is a Kubernetes control plane that classifies every tenant's workload live and plans replicas, semantic-cache budget, and model tier together — one joint optimization, every 10 seconds, per tenant, under budget and fairness guardrails.

Same tenants, same traffic, same cluster: stock Kubernetes autoscaling spends $4.53 and over-provisioning spends $37.16. PolyForge spends $3.42 — 25% less than the norm — while violating SLOs less and serving 1.7× more AI traffic from cache.

How it works

A classifier turns each tenant's telemetry into one of five workload classes every 10 s. A model-predictive planner then solves, jointly for all tenants:

min  α·cost + β·SLO violation + γ·(1−Jain)

subject to per-tenant budgets and cluster capacity. A Kubernetes operator applies the plan under guardrails — and when a tenant is flagged as a noisy neighbor, the planner shrinks it instead of feeding the interference.

Why the numbers hold

  • 1,800-run matrix + 500 ablations, zero flakes; 15/15 random replays reproduce bit-identically.
  • No straw men: every baseline grid-search-tuned on PolyForge's own objective; sweeps committed.
  • Honest stats: paired-by-cell tests, Holm-corrected gates, limitations stated (simulator-controlled runs; fairness term needs live interference to test).
  • One-command reproduction from experiment YAML to figures.

It's also a security result

A shared semantic cache — what most multi-tenant LLM deployments run — is a cross-tenant covert channel: a fast response tells an attacker that someone else recently asked a similar question. That's membership inference over private prompts, from response time alone. PolyForge's per-tenant cache was already the defense; the novel piece is quantifying the leak and the cost of closing it.
