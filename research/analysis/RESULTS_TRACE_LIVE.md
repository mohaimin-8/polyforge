## TL-H1 / TL-H2 — does the live ordering match the simulator's?

| id | metric | live | sim | agree? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TL-H1** | `total_cost_usd` | `jcac` > `hpa` | `jcac` > `hpa` | **yes** |
| **TL-H2** | `mean_violation` | `jcac` = `hpa` | `jcac` = `hpa` | **VACUOUS** |

**TL-H1 PASS** — on `total_cost_usd`, live ranks `jcac` > `hpa` and the simulator ranks it `jcac` > `hpa`.

**TL-H2 VACUOUS** — on `mean_violation`, live ranks `jcac` = `hpa` and the simulator ranks it `jcac` = `hpa`.

**This is not a PASS.** Both substrates report `mean_violation` identical across the two arms, so there was no ordering to transfer and the comparison could not have failed. At this cell's arrival rate the metric simply does not discriminate. Recorded as VACUOUS, and it supports no claim.

## What this does and does not license

On real trace demand, the live cluster ranked `jcac` and `hpa` as the simulator did, on both scored metrics. That is the whole claim. It does **not** license 'the simulator is validated', does not transfer to arms that were not run, and does not make the injected tier latencies real.

**W4 is closed either way**: the sentence *"no real trace has ever driven a real cluster"* is no longer true of this project. Two arms ran 2.00 h each on real BurstGPT demand, on a real cluster, scored against a pre-registration.
