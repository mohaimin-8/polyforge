## What this means

**EP-H1a FAILS, and that is the headline finding of this record.** Against `hpa_fair` — the same reactive replica controller, with the LRU charge removed and its cache pre-sized to the level the joint controller itself converges to — PolyForge is **+0.8% on cost**, i.e. cost-neutral, not cheaper. The published **-36.0%** against `hpa` is reproduced exactly in this same campaign, so the difference is not run-to-run variation: **36.8 percentage points of the published cost headline are eviction accounting and a baseline cache pinned at 128 MB, not control.**

The honest restatement is that the cost claim against a *competently configured* reactive replica autoscaler does not hold, and any future statement of it must name the baseline's cache posture and eviction pricing.


### What survives

- **Cost against event-driven scaling holds.** vs `keda_fair`, -14.1% (d_z -0.440, p 3.83e-16), which carries no eviction charge and the same pre-sized cache. Queue-depth scaling alone leaves real money on the table; joint control does not.
- **SLO severity is materially better, and the saturating metric was hiding it.** On unbounded `mean_excess` PolyForge is 0.2143 against 0.4288 — roughly half the overshoot (d_z -0.435, p 6.38e-12) — while `mean_violation` shows only 0.0691 vs 0.0699. The metric the audit flagged as unable to distinguish *slow* from *off* was, on this evidence, understating the proposal's advantage rather than flattering it.
- **The cache-bearing cell still wins.** On `ai_cacheable`, -14.2% against `hpa_fair` — where a knob the baseline does not have is doing real work.

### What this costs the thesis

The contribution is no longer 'joint control is 44-50% cheaper than reactive autoscaling'. On this evidence it is: **joint control is cost-neutral against a well-configured replica autoscaler while attaining materially lower SLO overshoot, and is cheaper than event-driven scaling** — with the advantage concentrated in cells where the cache and tier knobs are load-bearing. That is a narrower and more defensible claim, and it was reached by the project's own audit before review rather than after it.

`tier=none` step share is reported above for every arm (7.3% for `jcac`, 0.0% for every reactive arm): the proposal buys part of its position by shedding AI service, and that is now a first-class column rather than something a saturating metric absorbs.
