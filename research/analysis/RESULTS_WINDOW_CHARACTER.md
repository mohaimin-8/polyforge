## W2 — the split

A window is counted as *shedding* when jcac's `tier_none_step_share` exceeds 0.001 — i.e. it dropped AI service on at least some tenant-steps.


| trace | class | windows | jcac cheaper | mean cost delta |

| Azure LLM 2024 | sheds AI | 43 | 41 | -4.89 |
| Azure LLM 2024 | does not shed | 29 | 9 | +0.68 |

On BurstGPT the split is near-total: jcac is cheaper in **41 of 45** shedding windows and **0 of 51** non-shedding ones. Rank correlation between `tier_none_step_share` and the cost delta: **-0.826** (Pearson -0.958).


## W3 — the confound, stated plainly

jcac only sheds when it is loaded, so *sheds* and *high demand* are collinear and the split could be a relabelling of load. The test is whether the two classes' demand ranges overlap.


| trace | shedding demand range | non-shedding demand range | overlap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| BurstGPT v2.0 | 20.97 .. 574.14 | 2.50 .. 44.31 | **yes** |
| Azure LLM 2024 | 22.32 .. 89.86 | 3.17 .. 31.89 | **yes** |

Within the **non-shedding** BurstGPT windows, demand correlates **+0.822** with the cost delta — jcac grows steadily *more* expensive than the baseline as load rises, right up until it starts shedding. Within the **shedding** windows the shed share correlates **-0.979**: the more it sheds, the cheaper it gets.


## W5 — where the overshoot comes from (M2)

Unlike W2's split, this is **not** post-hoc: `jcac_nobudget` is a pre-registered arm from `PREREG_BUDGET_PARITY`, so lifting the per-tenant cap is a designed counterfactual. jcac's overshoot gap against `hpa_fair` splits into the part the cap causes and the part that survives without it.


| component | mean excess | share of the gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| total gap (`jcac` - `hpa_fair`) | 0.504588 | 100.0% |
| **budget-induced** (`jcac` - `jcac_nobudget`) | 0.400933 | **79.5%** |
| **genuine control error** (`jcac_nobudget` - `hpa_fair`) | 0.103656 | **20.5%** |

Crossed with W2's classes:


| class | windows | total gap | budget-induced | genuine |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sheds AI | 45 | 1.076453 | 0.855323 | 0.221130 |
| does not shed | 51 | 0.000002 | 0.000000 | 0.000002 |

**In the non-shedding windows jcac's overshoot gap is 0.000002** — indistinguishable from the fair baseline. The entire SLO penalty lives in the shedding windows, and within them roughly 79% of it is the per-tenant cap forcing AI to be shed rather than control error. Rank correlation between the induced component and the shed share: **+0.986**.


### The two findings joined

W2 says the cost advantage lives in 45 shedding windows and is absent from the other 51. W5 says the SLO penalty lives in the same shedding windows and is absent from the same others. **They are one trade, not two findings to reconcile:** where PolyForge sheds AI it is much cheaper and much worse on overshoot; where it does not shed it is neither. Four fifths of that overshoot is a constraint no replica-only controller can satisfy at all, which is the feasibility result `RESULTS_BUDGET_PARITY.md` reports.


## W4 — what this does and does not license

**Does.** It makes one mechanism measurable at window level: on BurstGPT, jcac's cost advantage and its SLO penalty are the same event. Cost win and severity loss are not two findings to be reconciled — they are one trade, visible window by window. That is what `RESULTS_BUDGET_PARITY.md` asserted in prose, now with a number attached.

**Does not.** It does not rescue TP-H1 or BP-H1, which stay FAILED as scored. It does not establish that shedding *causes* the cost advantage — the features were picked after the fact and the confound in W3 is real even where the ranges overlap. And it does not generalise beyond these two traces.

**The pre-registration it motivates**, stated here so it cannot be reverse-engineered from a later result: *on a trace held out from this analysis, the sign of the per-window cost delta between `jcac` and `hpa_fair` is predicted by `tier_none_step_share` alone at better than some threshold fixed in advance.* Until that is run, the split above is a description of two traces and nothing more.
