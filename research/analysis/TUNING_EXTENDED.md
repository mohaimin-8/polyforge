## Reading

- **The published bounds were not a formality.** `tune.py` bounded every grid on the ground that J falls monotonically toward static over-provisioning, which `static` covers. On the same slice HPA's J falls from 0.55488 (ρ = 0.3, the published edge) to 0.49994 (-9.9%) before it turns, while `static` never scores below 3.697 at any size: the excluded region holds the baselines' best settings, and `static` does not stand in for it.
- **The largest move is `gptcache`**: 7.36209 → 3.03179 (-58.8%). Every published comparison against it ran against a setting this far from its own optimum.
- **Against re-tuned baselines JCAC no longer wins every size on the tuning slice.** small: best `hpa` 0.51362 vs JCAC 0.53764. JCAC keeps its lead on medium, large.
- **VTC-replica**: within the published medium grid (ρ 0.3–0.8) its scores equal HPA's at every value, so its fair-division rule never engaged there and its published tuned value is HPA's. Once the pool binds it does engage (small, medium, large).
- **The comparator arms, re-tuned as the harness runs them**, beat JCAC on the tuning slice in: `hpa_fair` at small (-15.0%), `keda_fair` at small (-13.9%). Their re-tuned values are frozen in `eval/baselines/tuned_arms.yaml` and read only by the `*_retuned` arms.
- **What this does and does not change.** The tuning slice is 12 runs per setting on seeds disjoint from every campaign; it locates optima, it does not test hypotheses. It tunes the bare controllers as `tune.py` does; the confirmatory comparators wrap them (`hpa_fair` and `keda_fair` add a 512 MB cache without the LRU cost penalty), which is why the arm table tunes them as the harness runs them. Every published comparison ran against the bounded optima, and `RESULTS_FAIR_J.md`'s tie with `hpa_fair` is a tie with HPA at ρ = 0.3. Whether JCAC's FAIR_J results survive re-tuned comparators is a new confirmatory question on fresh seeds; this record only establishes that it has to be asked.
