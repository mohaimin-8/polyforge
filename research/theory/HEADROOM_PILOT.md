# Reference points for LEVEL2's main matrix (pilot, exploratory)

Generated by `research/theory/headroom_pilot.py`. Programme 2, WP1. Objective: LEVEL's J_L (every refused request charged the small tier's price). Each world is one design point and replication of `matrix_level2`; the arms' values are their registered runs, which saw the same demand (shared seeds).

- LB0: the cheapest state per tenant and step, no move limits, no caps (below every controller).
- LB1: the best path per tenant under the planner's move limits (+-2 replicas, +-1 cache level), no caps.
- LB2: LB1 with the cluster caps dualised (Lagrangian, subgradient ascent). The lower bounds omit the fairness term.
- UB: the best capped-feasible plan found (Lagrangian heuristic plus block-coordinate improvement) that starts within one move of the simulator's initial state (2 replicas, 128 MB), scored with the full objective. The capped clairvoyant optimum from that start lies in [LB2, UB].
- Static: the best fixed state per tenant inside an equal share of the caps, chosen with hindsight and held from the first step (as the simulator's static arm is), full objective.
- Regret of an arm over a cut: (mean arm - mean LB2) / (mean static - mean LB2); 0 is the lower bound, 1 the hindsight-static plan, above 1 worse than it.

Both references are clairvoyant: they know each world's realised demand, Poisson noise included. They bound what any controller could do; no deployable controller attains them.

## Checks

- Worlds: 300; ordering LB0 <= LB1 <= LB2 <= UB holds in 300.
- Arm runs below LB0 (impossible unless the arrays are wrong): 0 of 2100.
- Joint-controller runs below LB1: 0 of 600; of those, runs that never used the move-breaking fallback (where it would be impossible): 0.
- Arm runs below UB (a registered arm did better than the best plan found): 70 of 2100.
- Bound gap (UB - LB2) / (static - LB2): median 11.6%, worlds under 10%: 43% (the plan asks for at least 80%). Without the fairness term in UB (the term LB2 cannot see): median 4.5%, under 10%: 66%.

## The joint controller's distance to the references (means over the worlds)

| reference | J_L | minus joint search |
|---|---:|---:|

| best per-layer arm (by its mean) | 0.3999 | -0.0059 |
| static chosen on the noise-free demand curve, run on realised demand | 0.3699 | -0.0359 |
| hindsight static (chosen on realised demand) | 0.3647 | -0.0411 |
| plan made on the noise-free demand curve, run on realised demand | 0.3525 | -0.0532 |
| clairvoyant plan found (knows the noise too; best of both plans) | 0.3286 | -0.0772 |
| lower bound (LB2) | 0.3093 | -0.0965 |

The plan made on the noise-free curve beats the heuristic's plan for the realised world in 0% of worlds; the best of the two is a valid upper bound on the capped clairvoyant optimum. The distance from joint search to the curve plan is what a perfect forecast of the demand curve could buy; the further distance to the clairvoyant plan is the price of the Poisson noise.

Caveats: one plant (the published one) and one objective; the static reference is an equal share, not the best unequal fixed allocation; the clairvoyant references know the realised Poisson noise, so part of every gap to them cannot be forecast.
