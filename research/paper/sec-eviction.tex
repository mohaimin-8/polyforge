\section{Cost-Aware Semantic Cache Eviction}
\label{sec:eviction}

\subsection{Problem}
Semantic caches for LLM serving (GPTCache~\cite{gptcache2023},
MeanCache~\cite{meancache2024}, SCALM~\cite{scalm2024}) gate admission by
embedding similarity but evict with classical policies --- LRU or LFU ---
that assume every entry is equally expensive to recompute. In a
multi-tier gateway this is false by orders of magnitude: at the measured
tier prices (Table~\ref{tab:inference-bench}), a 900-token quality-tier
completion costs ${\sim}500\times$ a 300-token local-tier one. Evicting
by recency alone discards dollars.

Semantic caches additionally violate a core assumption of classical
eviction analysis: entries are not independent. If two cached prompts lie
within the admission threshold of each other, either can serve the
other's hits --- evicting one of a dense pair is nearly free, while
evicting an isolated entry forfeits its future hits entirely. No
classical policy observes this structure.

\subsection{Algorithm}
Every entry $e$ carries $(c_e, h_e, t^{store}_e, t^{last}_e, d_e)$:
recompute cost in dollars, hit count, store and last-access times, and
the embedding similarity to its nearest cached neighbor at store time.
At eviction time $t$, each entry is scored
%
\begin{equation}
  w(e) \;=\; L \;+\; \underbrace{c_e}_{\text{cost}}
  \times \underbrace{\frac{h_e + 1}{1 + (t - t^{store}_e)/\tau_h}}_{\text{hit rate}}
  \times \underbrace{2^{-(t - t^{last}_e)/\tau_s}}_{\text{staleness}}
  \times \underbrace{\frac{1}{\varepsilon + d_e}}_{\text{isolation}}
  \label{eq:eviction-weight}
\end{equation}
%
and the minimum-$w$ entry is evicted; $L$ is the GreedyDual inflation
value~\cite{cao1997greedydual}, updated to the victim's weight on every
eviction, so long-resident entries must keep re-earning their place. The
hit-rate term is Laplace-smoothed ($+1$) so fresh expensive entries
survive long enough to prove themselves, and normalized by lifetime in
units of $\tau_h$ so hoarded frequency decays --- the failure mode that
costs LFU $-199\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:eviction}. The isolation term is
deliberately bounded: with $\varepsilon = 0.5$ an isolated entry
($d_e{=}0$) outweighs a fully dense one ($d_e{=}1$) by at most
$3\times$, so substitutability breaks ties between economically
comparable entries but never outvotes measured hit economics. Defaults:
$\tau_h = 1$h, $\tau_s = 6$h.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cost-aware eviction (per tenant)}
\label{alg:eviction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{on store}$(e)$: record $(c_e, d_e)$;
  $h_e \gets 0$; $t^{store}_e, t^{last}_e \gets \text{now}$;
  $L_e \gets L$
\State \textbf{on hit}$(e)$: $h_e \gets h_e + 1$;
  $t^{last}_e \gets \text{now}$; $L_e \gets L$
\While{$|\text{cache}| > \text{capacity}$}
  \State $v \gets \arg\min_{e} w(e)$
    \Comment{Eq.~\ref{eq:eviction-weight}, $O(n)$ scan}
  \State $L \gets w(v)$; evict $v$
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Complexity.} The victim scan is $O(n)$ per eviction over
per-tenant caches of $10^3$--$10^4$ entries: measured p99 full-request
overhead is ${\sim}1$ms (Table~\ref{tab:eviction}), well inside the 5ms
budget. A lazy-heap variant would amortize to $O(\log n)$ at the price
of stale weights; at these cache sizes the scan is preferable.

\textbf{Isolation.} Capacity and eviction state are per tenant. A global
budget would let one tenant's flood evict another's expensive entries
--- a cross-tenant interference channel, verified excluded by test.

\subsection{Evaluation on synthetic LMSYS-shaped workloads}
The six policies run over one seeded stream (200k requests, 20k-prompt
universe, Zipf($1.07$) popularity, three topic-drift phases,
near-duplicate clusters of four paraphrases, tier mix 70/25/5 local/fast/
quality) in two capacity regimes. \emph{These are pipeline-validation
numbers}: the real-trace benchmark on LMSYS-Chat-1M replays
(\S\ref{sec:traces}) is the deciding experiment and may reorder the
close finishers.

\textbf{Reading the result honestly.} Cost-aware strictly dominates
every policy deployed in practice by semantic caches today --- LRU,
GPTCache's default, ARC --- by 24--38\% in dollars per request, and
demolishes LFU, whose undecayed frequency hoards the dead phases'
head. Against GDSF, the strongest classical cost-frequency policy, the
synthetic result is a statistical tie ($\pm 1$pp, lead trading with
scale). This is expected: GDSF already prices cost and frequency, and
the two terms GDSF lacks --- staleness under drift and semantic
substitutability --- are precisely the ones a synthetic exact-match
simulator undervalues. The cluster-serving model here credits density
conservatively (lexicographic representative, no partial similarity);
real LMSYS paraphrase traffic is where the isolation term differentiates,
which is why the real-trace replay is the paper's deciding experiment.
The claim this section stands on: \emph{cost-awareness is necessary}
(every cost-blind policy loses by ${\geq}24\%$) \emph{and the proposed
weight matches the best known cost-aware heuristic while adding the
semantic terms that exact-match simulation cannot reward}.
