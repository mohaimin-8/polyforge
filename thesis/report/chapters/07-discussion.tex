\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

This chapter confronts the objections a competent examiner should raise ---
each stated in its strongest form, answered with committed evidence, and
never softened. The full pre-answered set is maintained in
\evfile{docs/DEFENSE\_QA.md}; the load-bearing ones follow.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:disc-validity}

\paragraph{``Your headline numbers come from your own simulator.''}
True, and framed that way everywhere: decision quality under a stated system
model. Three things bound the gap. First, \emph{calibration direction}: the
congestion form was fitted against a real inference server and the model
over-penalizes high utilization ($a{=}0.86$ vs.\ assumed $1.0$) --- it errs
against \polyforge{}'s lean postures; the GPU tier bench confirms tier
ordering on real hardware. Second, \emph{real data}: the cost and
forecasting claims are restated on replayed BurstGPT demand ($n{=}96$
confirmatory) and the cache claims are measured on real LMSYS prompts.
Third, the \emph{live ordinal check}: the kind-cluster experiment asks only
whether the simulator's ranking reproduces, with the HPA arm verified and
the \jcac{} arm gated behind an executable actuation check. What is not
claimed: absolute dollars or latencies transferring to any production
fleet.

\paragraph{``You designed the v3 overload cells so your system would
win.''} The cells were sized by arithmetic on committed model constants,
pinned by unit tests, not tuned by trial runs. Three protections: the
pre-registration named the sole treatment and hypotheses before any run; a
same-envelope control cell (\texttt{ramp\_gentle}) exists where the alleged
advantage should not appear --- and it does not; and the confirmatory H1$'$
still \emph{failed}. A designed regime with an honest null inside it is
evidence of measurement, not of rigging.

\paragraph{``$p=10^{-33}$? Really?''} At hundreds of paired seeded simulator
runs, tiny p-values measure the simulator's determinism, not the effect. The
citation unit is the paired effect size with a bootstrap CI
(Section~\ref{sec:method-stats}); p-values appear once, as prereg gate
outcomes.

\paragraph{``Your SLO story moved the goalposts.''} The ledger says
otherwise, and the ledger is the answer: thirteen pre-registrations pushed
before their runs, two confirmatory SLO failures published as failures, one
mechanism confirmation, and a stopping rule forbidding a third attempt. The
final SLO claim --- parity at $-43\ldots{-48\%}$ cost --- is what survived,
not what was hoped for. Goalpost-moving is invisible when it happens in
private; here it happened in public, in the git history, where it can be
diffed.

\paragraph{``Were the baselines actually tuned, or strawmen?''}
Grid-searched on the paper's own composite objective, per baseline, sweeps
committed. The grids are bounded at a vendor-sane envelope because $J$
improves monotonically toward over-provisioning --- an unbounded grid would
tune every baseline into a cost blowout and \emph{flatter} \polyforge{}.
Iso-cost variants remove the ``it just spends less'' objection, and their
gate is reported FAIL-honest where it fails.

\section{Construct scope}

\paragraph{Fairness altitude.}
\label{sec:disc-fairness-altitude}
Jain's index over SLO satisfaction is not fairness as OSDI defines it: VTC
and successors define \emph{service} fairness at token granularity inside a
continuous-batching engine. \polyforge{}'s fairness is capacity allocation
across tenants at the control plane, the claims are scoped to that altitude,
and the \texttt{vtc\_replica} baseline is a replica-level transplant of
VTC's least-service-first idea, stated as such. Beating it on Jain says
\polyforge{} allocates capacity more evenly at lower cost --- not that it
schedules tokens more fairly. Token-level fair schedulers would run
\emph{inside} each replica pool \polyforge{} sizes; the two are
complementary.

\paragraph{Two economies in one cost model.} Replica-hours are priced as
infrastructure (\$0.048/replica-hr; the live arm meters real
replica-seconds) while model-tier calls are priced at market API ratios
(1:10:100). A self-hosting operator should read the tier ratios as relative
GPU-time costs --- the tier bench measured 1:1.5:16.6 latency ratios on real
hardware: same ordering, compressed scale, disclosed. Spot pricing, MIG
partitioning, and fractional GPUs are below the model's altitude and named
out of scope. Reconfiguration friction is not ignored: the realism ablation
bills startup lag and cold caches; multi-minute large-model pulls exceed
what it models, and the limitation says so.

\paragraph{Cache hits that are wrong answers.} Anticipated and measured
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-cache}): precision with strata and a
per-1k-incorrect-hits figure next to the savings, plus the stochasticity
ceiling that bounds what the proxy can say. No ``quality-adjusted dollar''
is invented, because pricing a wrong answer is application-specific.
Staleness and TTL are out of scope and listed as such.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limits}

\paragraph{The single load-bearing claim.} Breadth invites the question of
what one thing this thesis proves. The load-bearing claim is narrow and
falsifiable: \emph{under a stated, partially-calibrated system model, jointly
controlling replicas, cache, and model tier across a tenant portfolio yields
strictly better cost-at-fixed-fairness decision quality than any single-knob
reactive baseline tuned on the same objective, without a fairness or ---
outside the overload regime --- SLO regression.} Everything else (the
real-trace replays, the forecasting decomposition, the cache and isolation
measurements, the scaling and chaos campaigns) is supporting or scope-bounding
evidence for that claim, not a co-equal contribution. The limitations below
are read against that one claim.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Substrate.} Headline numbers are simulator decision quality;
        live validation is ordinal-only, and its pre-registered check
        recorded a disagreement --- live parity between the arms at the
        replica-only projection (Section~\ref{sec:eval-live}). The
        simulator's ranking is therefore \emph{unconfirmed} live, and the
        thesis claims stand on the simulator and real-data-replay substrates
        alone.
  \item \textbf{The live figure exercises one knob, and that knob did not
        separate the arms.} The live data plane burns fixed CPU per request
        kind, so cache and tier are inert there; the frozen caption states
        this, and the measured live parity in \texttt{ai\_cacheable} ---
        where the sim's separation flows through the cache economy --- is
        the disclosure made concrete. A live substrate with real cache and
        tier levers --- the joint controller's central novelty, which no live
        run has yet exercised in full --- is the named closure, now
        pre-registered
        (\evfile{research/analysis/PREREG\_WAVE4\_LIVE\_PLANE.md}) and gated
        only on a GPU-capable host.
  \item \textbf{Latency is p95, request-level.} The simulator's tail beyond
        p95 is not credible and is not reported; token-level dynamics (TTFT,
        decode-length tails, head-of-line blocking under continuous
        batching) are outside the model. The live path can report any
        percentile; p99 live restatement is future work, not silently
        substituted.
  \item \textbf{Trace scope.} Demand claims rest on BurstGPT plus the Azure
        LLM 2024 release; prompt claims on LMSYS-Chat-1M. One
        generalization failure is already published (the seasonal
        non-transfer), which is the strongest available evidence that the
        remaining claims are scoped honestly. FaaS/VM traces were
        deliberately descoped as non-LLM demand.
  \item \textbf{Tenant count.} Eight tenants per cluster is the studied
        composition factor; the planner's measured scaling (exponent 1.45,
        timeout crossover at 128 tenants) bounds, but does not remove, the
        gap to production tenant populations. Coordination costs outside
        the planner are unmeasured.
  \item \textbf{Production scale.} SageServe-class fleets serve more in a
        day than the entire replay; the 10.63M-request demand replay is the
        honest analog at decision level, and no stronger claim is made.
  \item \textbf{Tier-price sensitivity: measured, and the win survives.}
        The headline matrix prices tiers at API ratios (1:10:100); the GPU
        bench measured 1:1.516:16.64 (same ordering, compressed scale). The
        pre-registered rerun of the full 1{,}800-run matrix at that measured
        GPU corner (\evfile{research/analysis/RESULTS\_TIER\_RATIO.md}) holds:
        the cost win against all five baselines survives (TR-H1 PASS 5/5) and
        so does composite $J$ (TR-H2 PASS 5/5), and given cheap mid-tier
        inference the joint controller \emph{re-plans} into it (mid-tier steps
        $1.7\% \to 13.7\%$) and still wins. The headline still runs on the
        published economy for bit-reproducibility; this rerun is the
        sensitivity evidence, not a replacement.
  \item \textbf{The calibration story is asymmetric --- and now quantified
        both ways.} The congestion fit errs against \polyforge{}
        ($a{=}0.86$), while the simulator's assumed cache curve was
        \emph{optimistic} relative to the measured $h(K)$ ($h_{\max}{=}0.285$
        vs.\ the assumed $0.85$). The headline matrix ran on the assumed curve
        (constants frozen for bit-reproducibility), so the pre-registered
        rerun under the \emph{measured} curve
        (\evfile{research/analysis/RESULTS\_HK\_ADOPTION.md}) is the
        differential-effect measurement: pessimistic for every cache-using
        system, ours included --- realized hit rate halves ($0.106 \to
        0.051$), the cache-centric baseline is devalued most, and the joint
        controller stops paying for cache it can no longer use ($362 \to 136$
        MB) --- yet HK-H1 ($J$) and HK-H2 (cost) both PASS 5/5. The margin
        against HPA narrows ($-27.5\% \to -19.4\%$ aggregate) but does not
        close: the advantage was never primarily cache-driven.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration is anchored in git forensics}, not an
        external registry: protocols were pushed before runs, and
        amendments were self-adjudicated. The strongest counter-evidence is
        the record itself --- two failed confirmatory hypotheses and five
        further negatives published in full.
  \item \textbf{Per-tenant forecasting is supported one aggregation level
        down, not at true per-tenant granularity.} The three real traces are
        aggregate workload streams, not per-tenant SaaS demand. The
        extrapolation is now measured one level down
        (\evfile{research/analysis/PSEUDO\_TENANT.md}): the same traces split
        by Model\,$\times$\,Log-Type into 19 sub-streams reproduce the
        boundary with zero counterexamples (PT-H1), every periodic sub-stream
        shows the seasonal win (PT-H2), and three different forecasters win
        across sub-streams (PT-H3) --- exactly what a pluggable per-tenant
        forecasting layer exists to exploit. True per-tenant SaaS series
        remain unavailable, and that limit stands.
  \item \textbf{Chaos measured in simulation; live chaos still deferred.}
        The failure-injection campaign ran in the scoring engine with
        controllers blind (\evfile{research/analysis/RESULTS\_CHAOS\_SIM.md},
        540/540): a dead planner for one minute still beats a \emph{healthy}
        HPA (CH-H1 PASS, $d_z{=}{-}1.69$), \jcac{} keeps its $J$ win under an
        identical 50\% replica kill (CH-H2 PASS), and a one-minute freeze
        costs \jcac{} \emph{less} than a frozen HPA --- a proactively-planned
        held configuration ages better than a reactively-lagged one. What
        remains deferred is the \emph{live} analogue (planner crash mid-burst,
        apiserver throttling on the kind cluster), pre-registered and
        runbook-ready (\evfile{docs/WAVE3\_LIVE\_RUNBOOK.md}).
  \item \textbf{The isolation result is now over-the-wire; its threat model
        stays narrow.} The membership-inference channel and its defense were
        executed against the real \texttt{cmd/ai-gateway} process over HTTP
        (\evfile{eval/results/security/RESULTS\_WIRE\_ATTACK.md}): under
        per-tenant isolation the attacker's membership AUC is $0.502$ (95\% CI
        $[0.384, 0.612]$), zero cross-tenant hits --- chance, on the real
        cache/HTTP path (WA-H1 PASS) --- while the deliberately insecure
        shared posture leaks perfectly on loopback (AUC $1.000$, all 50 victim
        secrets). Two honest scope limits remain: the substrate is the gateway
        process over loopback (no WAN RTT jitter, which would only
        \emph{weaken} the shared-posture leak, never the defense), and the
        deployed offline embedder is lexical, so the measured threat is
        exact-prompt membership --- the conservative, embedder-agnostic form.
        Membership inference is still the only threat class studied, and
        whether a \emph{shared} cache can be defended without forfeiting its
        hit-rate benefit (noise, bucketing, differential privacy) is an open
        frontier question the partition/padding/TTL sweep does not answer.
  \item \textbf{The cost win is measured against orchestration-layer
        baselines, not the serving-engine frontier.} \polyforge{} is a control
        plane; its $-70\%$ figure is against tuned reactive autoscalers
        (HPA/KEDA/FIRM) that over-provision, not against a well-provisioned
        2026 serving engine whose efficiency comes from disaggregated
        prefill/decode, continuous batching, and KV-cache management
        (llm-d, AIBrix, Dynamo). Those systems are the \emph{actuation} level
        the three knobs sit above, and the mapping is direct
        (RELATED\_WORK \S4); what \polyforge{} adds is the joint,
        dollar-accounted, fairness-aware decision across a tenant portfolio,
        which none of them optimize. But the fraction of the orchestration-level
        win that survives on top of an engine-level-optimized stack is
        untested and is named future work, not implied away.
  \item \textbf{The advantage is conditional on the operator's SLO
        tolerance.} SLO attainment is a published parity, not a win
        (v2 H1, v3 H1$'$ both FAIL), and the composite-$J$ advantage narrows
        under violation-first weightings --- the nine losses in the weight
        sweep all sit at $w_v{=}4$
        (\evfile{research/analysis/SENSITIVITY\_J.md}), the profile of a
        latency-strict operator. The honest positioning follows: \polyforge{}
        wins for cost-sensitive, SLO-tolerant tenant portfolios; an operator
        who prices a violation far above a dollar of spend sits outside the
        regime where the trade is favorable, and the thesis says so rather
        than averaging that operator away.
  \item \textbf{The evidence chain is self-contained.} Simulator, baselines,
        objective function, trace ETL, and analysis are one author's code on
        one machine; the GPU tier-bench replication reproduces the author's
        own kernel, and the pre-registration anchor is git-push forensics, not
        yet an external registry. Independent replication is what the archived
        artifact bundle and the OSF/Zenodo deposits exist to enable
        (\evfile{docs/RELEASE\_CHECKLIST.md}); until a third party re-runs the
        campaigns, the published nulls and the immutable commit history are the
        strongest available counter to the circularity this implies.
\end{itemize}

\section{The honest-nulls ledger as a contribution}

Eight published negatives travel with the wins: v2 H1, v3 H1$'$, the
underpowered first replay, the $\gamma$-term null, the seasonal
non-transfer, the structurally impossible raw per-metric gate, the cache
hit-quality result, and the live ordinal check (the simulator's $J$ winner
flips to live parity in both cells, published as measured). None was hidden;
several redirected the design (Holt as
recommended default; per-stream forecasting; per-tenant caches). The
methodological claim of this thesis --- pre-registration with published
nulls in a systems evaluation --- is independent of any performance number
and, in the surveyed literature, unique to this work.
