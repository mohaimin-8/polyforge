\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What was established}

This thesis set out to test whether per-layer local optima compose in
multi-tenant hybrid serving, and answered with a built system and a
pre-registered measurement campaign. The answer: they do not compose. A
controller that plans replicas, semantic-cache budget, and model tier
together beats every tuned per-layer baseline on the composite objective in
every campaign that tested it --- the 1{,}800-run matrix, two real-trace
replays, the 2026-stack concurrency arm, the tenant-scale slice, and an
offline-trained learned controller over the same action space --- and is
the only Pareto-undominated system in the matrix, non-dominated in 48 of
60 cells under any weighting. Removing jointness worsens the cost term
against the published layered comparator by $+2884\%$, and by a margin
$86\%$ smaller once that comparator's tier defect is repaired; the smaller
number is the composition gap made honest.

The cost \emph{percentage} that once headlined this thesis --- $-70\%$
against tuned reactive scalers on replayed real demand --- is withdrawn.
An audit after the campaigns closed found that every comparator carried an
eviction charge and a pinned cache no \jcac{} arm paid, and that only
\jcac{} was ever held to the per-tenant budget; re-scored under three
pre-registered corrections, the like-for-like advantage does not survive
its own rank tests. What survives is sharper than a percentage: the
per-tenant budget is a promise only a controller holding the tier knob can
keep, because capping a replica-only controller moves its tier spend by
exactly nothing. That is the thesis's cost claim.

Around that central result: an honest SLO account (parity at
$-43\ldots{-48\%}$ cost, a confirmed forecasting mechanism, two published
confirmatory failures, a stopping rule, and a severity reversal on one real
trace stated beside the advantage on the other); real-data cache results
with a quality denominator no surveyed cache paper publishes, and the
ceiling on that quality located in response stochasticity rather than
retrieval; fairness wins over two dedicated baselines at the stated
altitude with the controller's own fairness term honestly nulled; a
security frontier showing per-tenant cache partitioning dominates padding
and jitter defenses against a demonstrated AUC-0.88 timing side channel,
confirmed at chance over the wire; two construction-level guarantees on the
deployed solver, a single-tenant cost-separation result and its
multi-tenant extension computed exactly and falsified; live evidence at
three depths, including a three-knob live plane on which the published
controller lost to its own ablation and the corrected controller then beat
every arm at iso-fairness; and a working Kubernetes-native implementation
held to CI gates throughout.

The methodological result may outlast the numbers: forty-eight
pre-registrations pushed before their runs, immutable closed campaigns,
effect sizes over p-values, substrate separation, adjudication by new
record rather than by edit, a published honest-nulls ledger, and a
reproduction gate that re-derives every record, figure and caption from
the committed data --- demonstrated in a systems evaluation at thesis
scale, in public git history.

\section{Future work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A damper for the corrected controller.} The three-knob
        live plane (Section~\ref{sec:eval-live-plane}) is done: the
        corrected controller wins at iso-fairness but oscillates its
        replica count in a slow swing, and the obvious damper --- a dwell
        on reversals --- failed its pre-registered test. The next damper
        must act on the plant-estimate feedback (asymmetric fast-up /
        slow-down learning of the tier-latency offset), and it is a new
        controller design with a new pre-registration, not a knob.
  \item \textbf{The cost claim on an engine-optimized stack.} The
        feasibility result --- only a tier-holding controller meets the
        per-tenant budget --- was established against orchestration-layer
        reactive baselines. Whether, and how much of, the advantage
        survives on top of a serving engine that already batches,
        disaggregates and manages its KV cache is untested.
  \item \textbf{Token-level demand signals.} Swap the request-rate
        \texttt{DemandSource} for TTFT/queue-depth/KV-pressure signals from
        an llm-d-class engine, keeping the planner unchanged --- the
        designed migration path onto the 2026 substrate
        (Section~\ref{sec:bg-substrate}).
  \item \textbf{p99 live restatement.} The live path measures wall-time
        latency; extending the ordinal protocol to p99 removes the
        simulator's p95 scope limit.
  \item \textbf{Planner scaling past 128 tenants.} Planning cells,
        incremental fairness partial sums, and a faster inner loop are the
        named levers against the measured 1.45 growth exponent.
  \item \textbf{Adopting the empirical cache curve.} Feeding the measured
        $h(K)$ fit into the model constants is a new pre-registered
        experiment by the project's own rules --- deliberately left for one.
  \item \textbf{Prefix/KV-cache layers and token-level fairness.} Both sit
        below \polyforge{}'s altitude and compose with it; a joint
        portfolio-plus-engine study is the natural next thesis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing remark}

The strongest sentence this project can write is not a percentage. It is
that every percentage in it can be traced --- to a protocol committed before
the run, a script that regenerates it, and a git history that would have
exposed any other way of obtaining it. That standard is portable to any
systems evaluation, and it is the practice this thesis most hopes to see
reused.
